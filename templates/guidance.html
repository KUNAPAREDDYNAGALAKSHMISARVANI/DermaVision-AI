The ABCDE Rule for Mole Self-Examination

The ABCDE criteria are globally recognized visual markers used by dermatologists to identify suspicious moles or potential melanomas.

A

Asymmetry

One half of the spot or mole does not match the other half in shape or contour.

B

Border

The edges of the lesion are irregular, scalloped, blurred, ragged, or poorly defined.

C

Color

The color is uneven across the lesion, showing shades of brown, black, pink, red, white, or blue.

D

Diameter

The spot is larger than 6 millimeters across (about the size of a pencil eraser), though melanomas can be smaller.

E

Evolving

The mole is changing in size, shape, color, elevation, or exhibiting new symptoms like bleeding or itching.

Comprehensive Medical Resources & Care Guidelines

Sun Protection Guidelines

  • Apply broad-spectrum SPF 30+ sunscreen daily.
  • Reapply every 2 hours when outdoors or after swimming.
  • Avoid direct peak sunlight between 10:00 AM and 04:00 PM.
  • Wear UV-blocking sunglasses and wide-brimmed hats.

Skin Self-Examination Tips

  • Examine your skin once a month in a well-lit room.
  • Use a full-length mirror and hand mirror for hard-to-see areas.
  • Check scalp, palm, soles, fingernails, and behind knees.
  • Take clear photos to monitor mole evolution over time.

Daily Skincare Best Practices

  • Use gentle, fragrance-free cleansers to preserve moisture.
  • Hydrate skin daily with non-comedogenic moisturizers.
  • Avoid harsh scrubbing over sensitive skin lesions.
  • Stay hydrated by drinking adequate water throughout the day.

Red Flag Warning Signs

  • Moles that rapidly change shape within weeks.
  • Non-healing skin ulcers or persistent open sores.
  • New growths appearing after age 40.
  • Lesions accompanied by fever, swelling, or pus.
